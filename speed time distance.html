Time, Distance, and Speed Explained

Speed tells us how fast something is moving, time tells us how long it takes, and distance tells us how far it travels. These three ideas are connected by the simple rule distance = speed × time, so higher speed or longer time means more distance covered. This makes motion easy to understand and apply in everyday problems like travel, sports, and planning.

SPEED=DISTANCE/TIME

RELATIVE SPEED

Relative speed is the speed of one object compared with another. If two objects move in the same direction, their relative speed is the difference of their speeds. If they move in opposite directions, it is the sum of their speeds.

Question 1

Q1. A cyclist travels 120 km in 3 hours.

What is the speed of the cyclist?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Recall the formula for Speed.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

This means:

  • Distance = How far we travel.
  • Time = How long the journey takes.
  • Speed = How much distance is covered every hour.

Step 2: Identify the values given in the question.

Distance = 120 km
Time = 3 hours

Step 3: Substitute these values into the formula.

Speed = 120 ÷ 3

= 40 km/h

Step 4: Check the units.

Distance is measured in kilometres (km) and time is measured in hours (h).

Therefore, the speed is written as:

40 km/h

✅ Correct Answer: c) 40 km/h

💡 Quick Tip:

Whenever a question asks "What is the speed?", immediately think:

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Just divide the distance by the time and don't forget to include the unit km/h.

Question 2

Q2. A train travels at a constant speed of 80 km/h for 5 hours.

How far does it travel?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Recall the formula for finding distance.

Distance = Speed × Time

This means:

  • Speed tells us how many kilometres are covered every hour.
  • Time tells us for how many hours the object keeps moving.
  • Distance is the total journey covered.

Step 2: Identify the given values.

Speed = 80 km/h
Time = 5 hours

Step 3: Substitute the values into the formula.

Distance = 80 × 5

= 400 km

Step 4: Understand why.

If the train travels 80 km every hour, then:

  • After 1 hour → 80 km
  • After 2 hours → 160 km
  • After 3 hours → 240 km
  • After 4 hours → 320 km
  • After 5 hours → 400 km

So multiplying the speed by the time gives the total distance travelled.

✅ Correct Answer: c) 400 km

💡 Quick Tip:

Whenever a question asks "How far?" or "What distance?", immediately think:

Distance = Speed × Time

Remember the units:

  • Speed → km/h
  • Time → hours
  • Distance → km

Question 3

Q3. A bus covers 270 km at a constant speed of 45 km/h.

How long does the journey take?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Recall the formula for finding time.

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

This means:

  • Distance = Total journey covered.
  • Speed = Distance travelled every hour.
  • Time = Number of hours required to complete the journey.

Step 2: Identify the values given.

Distance = 270 km
Speed = 45 km/h

Step 3: Substitute the values into the formula.

Time = 270 ÷ 45

= 6 hours

Step 4: Let's understand why.

The bus covers 45 km every hour.

So:

  • After 1 hour → 45 km
  • After 2 hours → 90 km
  • After 3 hours → 135 km
  • After 4 hours → 180 km
  • After 5 hours → 225 km
  • After 6 hours → 270 km ✅

Therefore, the journey takes 6 hours.

✅ Correct Answer: b) 6 hours

💡 Quick Tip:

Whenever a question asks "How long?" or "How much time?", immediately think:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Remember the units:

  • Distance → kilometres (km)
  • Speed → kilometres per hour (km/h)
  • Time → hours (h)

🧠 Memory Trick

Think of the letters:

D = S × T

From this one formula, you can derive all three:

  • Distance = Speed × Time
  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time
  • Time = Distance ÷ Speed

If you remember D = S × T, you'll never forget the other two formulas.

Question 4

Q4. A delivery drone flies at a constant speed of 60 km/h.

How much distance will it cover in 2 hours 30 minutes?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Recall the formula.

Distance = Speed × Time

Step 2: Write the given values.

  • Speed = 60 km/h
  • Time = 2 hours 30 minutes

Step 3: Convert the time into hours.

30 minutes = 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5 hour

Therefore,

2 hours 30 minutes = 2.5 hours

Step 4: Apply the formula.

Distance = 60 × 2.5

= 150 km

Why is conversion necessary?

The speed is given in kilometres per hour (km/h), so the time must also be in hours. Always convert minutes into hours before using the formula.

✅ Correct Answer: c) 150 km

💡 Quick Tip

  • 15 minutes = 0.25 hour
  • 30 minutes = 0.5 hour
  • 45 minutes = 0.75 hour
  • 60 minutes = 1 hour

Question 5

Q5. A wildlife photographer leaves a forest camp at 6:00 AM and drives towards a wildlife reserve at a constant speed of 54 km/h.

After travelling for 2 hours, he stops for 30 minutes to repair his camera before continuing at the same speed.

If the reserve is 189 km away from the camp, at what time does he reach the reserve?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Calculate the distance covered before stopping.

Distance = Speed × Time

= 54 × 2

= 108 km

Step 2: Find the remaining distance.

Remaining Distance = 189 − 108

= 81 km

Step 3: Calculate the time needed for the remaining journey.

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

= 81 ÷ 54

= 1.5 hours (1 hour 30 minutes)

Step 4: Add every part of the journey.

  • Driving before stop = 2 hours
  • Camera repair = 30 minutes
  • Remaining drive = 1 hour 30 minutes

Total Time = 2 h + 30 min + 1 h 30 min

= 4 hours

Step 5: Calculate the arrival time.

Start Time = 6:00 AM

Journey Time = 4 hours

Arrival Time = 10:00 AM

✅ Correct Answer: b) 10:00 AM

💡 Exam Tip

Whenever a question contains a stop, break, traffic delay, or repair time, remember that this time must also be included in the total journey time, even though no distance is covered during the stop.

Question 6

Q6. A supply train travels from City A to City B, a distance of 480 km.

Due to track maintenance, it travels the first 180 km at 60 km/h and the remaining distance at 75 km/h.

How long does the entire journey take?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Find the time taken for the first part of the journey.

Formula:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Time = 180 ÷ 60

= 3 hours

Step 2: Find the remaining distance.

Remaining Distance = 480 − 180

= 300 km

Step 3: Find the time taken for the remaining journey.

Time = 300 ÷ 75

= 4 hours

Step 4: Add both travel times.

Total Time = 3 + 4

= 7 hours

✅ Correct Answer: b) 7 hours

💡 Quick Tip

When a journey has different speeds, never divide the total distance by one of the speeds. Instead:

  1. Break the journey into parts.
  2. Calculate the time for each part separately.
  3. Add all the times together.

🧠 Exam Trick

Whenever you notice words like "first", "remaining", "then", or "after that", solve each section separately. This avoids one of the most common mistakes in Speed–Distance–Time questions.

Question 7

Q7. A cyclist plans to reach a competition 240 km away.

He rides at 48 km/h for the first 3 hours. He then reduces his speed to 40 km/h for the remaining distance.

How much total time does the journey take?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Find the distance covered during the first 3 hours.

Formula:

Distance = Speed × Time

Distance = 48 × 3

= 144 km

Step 2: Calculate the remaining distance.

Remaining Distance = 240 − 144

= 96 km

Step 3: Find the time required to cover the remaining distance.

Formula:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Time = 96 ÷ 40

= 2.4 hours

Since 0.4 hour = 0.4 × 60 = 24 minutes,

2.4 hours = 2 hours 24 minutes.

Step 4: Find the total journey time.

First part = 3 hours

Second part = 2 hours 24 minutes

Total Time = 5 hours 24 minutes

✅ Correct Answer: b) 5 h 24 min

💡 Quick Tip

Whenever your answer contains a decimal number of hours, convert the decimal part into minutes.

Multiply the decimal by 60.

Examples:

  • 0.5 hour = 30 minutes
  • 0.25 hour = 15 minutes
  • 0.4 hour = 24 minutes
  • 0.75 hour = 45 minutes

🧠 Exam Trick

Many students stop at 2.4 hours. In aptitude exams, if the options are in hours and minutes, always convert the decimal part into minutes before choosing the answer.

Question 8

During the first 120 km, it moves at 24 km/h while scanning rock samples. It then switches to exploration mode and travels the remaining distance at 36 km/h.

What is the total travel time?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Find the time taken for the first part of the journey.

Formula:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Time = 120 ÷ 24

= 5 hours

Step 2: Find the remaining distance.

Remaining Distance = 300 − 120

= 180 km

Step 3: Calculate the time for the remaining journey.

Time = 180 ÷ 36

= 5 hours

Step 4: Add both travel times.

First Part = 5 hours

Second Part = 5 hours

Total Time = 10 hours

✅ Correct Answer: b) 10 hours

💡 Quick Tip

Whenever a journey is divided into two or more parts with different speeds, always calculate the time for each part separately. Then add all the times together to get the total travel time.

🧠 Exam Trick

Words like "first", "remaining", "then", or "after that" usually mean the journey is split into multiple sections. Solve each section one by one instead of trying to use one formula for the entire journey.

It flies at 180 km/h for the first 2 hours. Due to severe weather, it slows to 120 km/h for the remaining distance.

How long does the complete journey take?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Find the distance covered during the first 2 hours.

Formula:

Distance = Speed × Time

Distance = 180 × 2

= 360 km

Step 2: Calculate the remaining distance.

Remaining Distance = 720 − 360

= 360 km

Step 3: Find the time needed for the remaining journey.

Formula:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Time = 360 ÷ 120

= 3 hours

Step 4: Calculate the total journey time.

First Part = 2 hours

Second Part = 3 hours

Total Time = 5 hours

✅ Correct Answer: b) 5 hours

💡 Quick Tip

If a question gives you the time for one part and the speed, first calculate the distance covered. Then use the remaining distance to solve the rest of the question.

🧠 Exam Trick

Don't rush to divide the total distance by one speed. Whenever the speed changes during a journey, break the journey into separate parts and solve each one individually before adding the times.

Question 10

Q10. A logistics company sends a truck carrying relief supplies to a village 350 km away.

The truck travels the first 140 km at 70 km/h. Heavy rainfall forces it to reduce its speed to 42 km/h for the remaining journey.

How long does the entire journey take?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Calculate the time taken for the first part of the journey.

Formula:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Time = 140 ÷ 70

= 2 hours

Step 2: Calculate the remaining distance.

Remaining Distance = 350 − 140

= 210 km

Step 3: Find the time taken for the remaining journey.

Time = 210 ÷ 42

= 5 hours

Step 4: Add the travel times.

First Part = 2 hours

Second Part = 5 hours

Total Time = 2 + 5 = 7 hours

✅ Correct Answer: b) 7 hours

💡 Why can't we use only one speed?

The truck does not travel at the same speed throughout the journey.

It travels:

  • 70 km/h during the first part.
  • 42 km/h during the remaining part.

Since the speed changes, the journey must be divided into separate sections. Calculate the time for each section individually and then add them together.

🧠 Exam Trick

Whenever you see words such as "first", "remaining", "after", "later", "then", or a reason for slowing down like traffic, rain, or roadwork, it usually means the journey is split into multiple parts.

👉 Solve each part separately and add the times at the end.

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Distance = Speed × Time
  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time
  • Time = Distance ÷ Speed

These three formulas are enough to solve most basic and moderate Speed–Distance–Time questions.

Question 11

Q11. A disaster relief convoy leaves City A at 6:30 AM to reach a flood-hit town 612 km away.

• It covers the first 30% of the journey at 72 km/h.
• It stops for 40 minutes to repair a damaged bridge.
• It then travels 180 km at 60 km/h.
• The remaining distance is completed at 90 km/h.

At what time does the convoy reach the town?






📖 Solution

30% of 612 = 183.6 km

Time₁ = 183.6 ÷ 72 = 2 h 33 min

Repair time = 40 min

Time₂ = 180 ÷ 60 = 3 h

Remaining distance = 612 − 183.6 − 180 = 248.4 km

Time₃ = 248.4 ÷ 90 = 2 h 46 min

Total journey time = 2 h 33 min + 40 min + 3 h + 2 h 46 min = 8 h 59 min

6:30 AM + 8 h 59 min = 3:29 PM

✅ Correct Answer: a) 3:29 PM

Exam Tip: When a journey has percentages, fixed distances, and halts, solve each section separately and add the times at the end.

Question 12

Q12. A high-speed research train must cover a total distance of 840 km.

• During the first 2 hours, it travels at 90 km/h.
• It then reduces its speed by 20% for the next 240 km because of maintenance work.
• After the maintenance zone, it increases its speed by 25% over its original speed for the remaining journey.

How long does the entire journey take?






📖 Solution

Original speed = 90 km/h

Step 1:

Distance in first 2 hours

= 90 × 2

= 180 km

Step 2:

Reduced speed = 90 × 80%

= 72 km/h

Time = 240 ÷ 72

= 3 h 20 min

Step 3:

Remaining distance

= 840 − 180 − 240

= 420 km

New speed = 90 × 125%

= 112.5 km/h

Time = 420 ÷ 112.5

= 3 h 44 min

Step 4:

  • 2 h
  • 3 h 20 min
  • 3 h 44 min

Total = 9 h 04 min

✅ Correct Answer: b) 9 h 04 min

💡 Exam Tip: A 20% reduction means multiply by 0.8. A 25% increase means multiply by 1.25. Don't subtract or add the percentage directly to the distance.

Question 13

Q13. A research expedition vehicle must travel a total distance of 600 km.

• During the first 40% of the journey, it travels at 80 km/h.
• During the next 30%, rough terrain forces it to slow down to 60 km/h.
• It completes the remaining distance at 100 km/h.

How long does the complete journey take?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Convert the percentages into distances.

  • 40% of 600 = 240 km
  • 30% of 600 = 180 km
  • Remaining = 180 km

Step 2: Calculate the time for each section.

240 ÷ 80 = 3 hours

180 ÷ 60 = 3 hours

180 ÷ 100 = 1.8 hours

0.8 hour = 48 minutes

Step 3: Add all the times.

  • 3 h
  • 3 h
  • 1 h 48 min

Total = 7 h 48 min

✅ Correct Answer: b) 7 h 50 min (closest option)

⚠️ Note: The exact answer is 7 h 48 min. In a real exam, the options should match exactly. The nearest option is shown here, but ideally the option should be 7 h 48 min.

💡 Exam Tip: Whenever percentages of a journey are given, convert them into actual distances before using any Speed–Distance–Time formula.

Question 14

Q14. A wildlife rescue team travels from a sanctuary to a veterinary hospital.

While carrying an injured animal, the ambulance covers the first 180 km at 60 km/h.

After safely handing over the animal, the empty ambulance returns along the same route at 90 km/h.

What is the average speed of the ambulance for the entire round trip?






📖 Solution

Many students make this mistake:

(60 + 90) ÷ 2 = 75 km/h ❌

This is incorrect because the ambulance spends different amounts of time travelling at each speed.

Step 1: Find the total distance.

180 + 180 = 360 km

Step 2: Find the total time.

Going:

180 ÷ 60 = 3 hours

Returning:

180 ÷ 90 = 2 hours

Total Time = 5 hours

Step 3:

Average Speed =

Total Distance ÷ Total Time

360 ÷ 5

= 72 km/h

✅ Correct Answer: a) 72 km/h

💡 CLAT Trap:

Never average two speeds directly unless the time spent at each speed is exactly the same.

When the distance is the same, always calculate Total Distance ÷ Total Time.

Question 15

Q15. A car travels from City A to City B, a distance of 240 km, at a constant speed of 60 km/h.

During the journey, it is forced to stop at four traffic signals, each lasting 6 minutes.

Ignoring all other delays, what is the average speed of the car for the entire journey?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Find the driving time.

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

240 ÷ 60 = 4 hours

Step 2: Find the total stopping time.

4 traffic signals × 6 minutes

= 24 minutes

24 minutes = 0.4 hour

Step 3: Calculate the total journey time.

4 + 0.4 = 4.4 hours

Step 4: Find the average speed.

Average Speed =

Total Distance ÷ Total Time

240 ÷ 4.4

= 54.55 km/h

✅ Correct Answer: c) 54.55 km/h

💡 CLAT Trap:

Many students choose 60 km/h because the car never changes its driving speed.

However, average speed depends on the total journey time, which includes both moving time and waiting time.

Whenever the words "stop", "signal", "delay", "waiting", or "halt" appear, include that time before calculating the average speed.

Question 16

Following continuous rainfall in three districts, the State Disaster Management Authority deployed a medical convoy from its central warehouse to a relief camp located 672 km away.

The convoy consisted of five trucks, but only one truck carried temperature-sensitive medicines. Since the medicines had to remain below a specified temperature, the truck could not exceed certain speeds during different phases of the journey.

For the first 192 km, the truck maintained a speed of 64 km/h. After reaching the state border, officials inspected all vehicles for 36 minutes before allowing them to continue.

The truck then travelled the next 240 km at 80 km/h. Heavy rainfall later forced the driver to reduce its speed to 60 km/h for the remaining distance.

The convoy left the warehouse at 6:45 AM.


At what time did the medicine truck reach the relief camp?






📖 Solution

Ignore the extra information.

The number of trucks and temperature-sensitive medicines do not affect the calculation.

First part:

192 ÷ 64 = 3 hours

Inspection halt = 36 minutes

Second part:

240 ÷ 80 = 3 hours

Remaining distance

672 − 192 − 240 = 240 km

Final part:

240 ÷ 60 = 4 hours

Total Time = 3 h + 36 min + 3 h + 4 h = 10 h 36 min

Departure = 6:45 AM

Arrival = 5:21 PM

✅ Correct Answer: b) 5:21 PM

💡 CLAT Reading Tip: Do not use every fact in a passage. Identify only the information related to distance, speed, time and stoppages. Ignore background details unless they affect the journey.

Question 17

The National Wildlife Rescue Authority transported a rare snow leopard from a conservation centre to a rehabilitation facility located 840 km away.

The transport team consisted of wildlife veterinarians, biologists and forest officers. Every 90 minutes, veterinarians checked the animal's vital signs while the vehicle was still moving. These medical inspections required no stoppage and therefore did not affect the travelling time.

The vehicle covered the first 240 km at 80 km/h. After reaching a forest checkpoint, officials inspected documents for 30 minutes.

The next 300 km were travelled at 75 km/h because of winding mountain roads. The remaining journey was completed at 100 km/h.

The vehicle departed the conservation centre at 5:45 AM.


At what time did the transport vehicle reach the rehabilitation facility?






📖 Solution

Ignore the misleading information.

The veterinary checks happened while the vehicle was moving, so they do not increase the journey time.

First part:

240 ÷ 80 = 3 hours

Checkpoint halt = 30 minutes

Second part:

300 ÷ 75 = 4 hours

Remaining distance:

840 − 240 − 300 = 300 km

Final part:

300 ÷ 100 = 3 hours

Total Time = 3 h + 30 min + 4 h + 3 h = 10 h 30 min

Departure = 5:45 AM

Arrival = 4:15 PM

✅ Correct Answer: None of the options

⚠️ The options above are intentionally inconsistent. The correct arrival time is 4:15 PM. In a real test, the options should match exactly.

Question 18

Following a powerful cyclone, two emergency response teams were dispatched from opposite ends of a damaged national highway connecting two districts. Communication towers had collapsed, making direct contact impossible. The objective was for both teams to meet as quickly as possible and exchange medical supplies before continuing towards the opposite district.

The total distance between the two emergency bases was 600 km.

Team Alpha left the western base travelling at a constant speed of 54 km/h.

At the same moment, Team Bravo departed from the eastern base travelling at 66 km/h.

Both teams travelled continuously without stopping. Although weather conditions changed during the journey, neither team reduced its speed.


When the two teams meet, how far has Team Alpha travelled from the western base?






📖 Solution

Since the teams move towards each other, use Relative Speed.

Relative Speed = 54 + 66 = 120 km/h

Time to Meet = 600 ÷ 120 = 5 hours

Distance travelled by Team Alpha = 54 × 5 = 270 km

✅ Correct Answer: b) 270 km

💡 CLAT Trap

Many students wrongly divide the total distance by 2 and answer 300 km. They only meet halfway when both teams travel at the same speed. Here, Team Bravo is faster, so Team Alpha travels less than half the total distance before they meet.

Question 19

Every morning, two express trains operate on the same railway corridor connecting New Delhi and Jaipur, which are 420 km apart. Passengers often wonder where the trains cross each other during their journey.

The Aravalli Express departs from New Delhi at 7:00 AM and maintains a constant speed of 60 km/h throughout the journey.

The Pink City Express departs from Jaipur exactly 30 minutes later at 7:30 AM and travels continuously at 96 km/h.

Both trains run without any unscheduled halts and maintain their respective speeds until they cross each other.


At what distance from New Delhi do the two trains cross each other?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Find the head start of the Aravalli Express.

30 minutes = 0.5 hour

Distance covered = 60 × 0.5 = 30 km

Step 2:

Remaining distance between the trains

420 − 30 = 390 km

Step 3:

Relative Speed = 60 + 96 = 156 km/h

Time after Pink City Express starts

390 ÷ 156 = 2.5 hours

Step 4:

Distance travelled by Aravalli after 7:30 AM

60 × 2.5 = 150 km

Total distance from New Delhi

30 + 150 = 180 km

✅ Correct Answer: b) 180 km

💡 CLAT Trap:

Many students immediately divide 420 by (60 + 96) and forget that the Aravalli Express had already travelled 30 km before the second train even started. Always account for the head start before applying relative speed.

Question 20

A sports academy has a circular athletics track with a circumference of 600 metres. During a morning practice session, two athletes, Aarav and Vihaan, start running simultaneously from the same starting point in the same direction. The coach wants to know when both runners will again be together at the starting point.

Aarav runs at a constant speed of 15 km/h, while Vihaan runs at 12 km/h. Both athletes maintain their speeds throughout the practice without taking any breaks.


⭐ Important Formula

Step 1: Convert the speed into the same unit as the track length (m/min).

Lap Time = Track Length ÷ Speed

Step 2: If runners start together and move in the same direction, then the first time they are together at the starting point is:

Required Time = LCM of the Lap Times


After how much time will both athletes be together at the starting point for the first time?






📖 Solution

Step 1: Convert speeds into metres per minute.

15 km/h = 15000 m/hour = 250 m/min

12 km/h = 12000 m/hour = 200 m/min

Step 2: Find the lap time of each runner.

Aarav

600 ÷ 250 = 2.4 minutes

Vihaan

600 ÷ 200 = 3 minutes

Step 3: Find the LCM of the lap times.

Aarav completes one lap every 2.4 minutes.

Vihaan completes one lap every 3 minutes.

The first common time at which both complete a whole number of laps is 12 minutes.

In 12 minutes,

Aarav completes 12 ÷ 2.4 = 5 laps

Vihaan completes 12 ÷ 3 = 4 laps

Therefore, both runners are together at the starting point after 12 minutes.

✅ Correct Answer: b) 12 minutes

💡 CLAT Tip:

Do not use the relative speed formula here. Relative speed tells you when one runner catches the other. This question asks when both runners are together at the starting point, so use the LCM of the lap times.
